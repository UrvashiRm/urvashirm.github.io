Competitive Benchmark: Centrepoint's Women's Store vs. Namshi, Myntra & Shein

An online merchandising audit of Centrepoint's Women's category pages — homepage, product listing, and product detail — against three competitors with very different strategies, to find where the shopping experience wins or loses a customer before checkout even comes into it.

Online Merchandising UX Benchmarking CLP / PLP / PDP Conversion Strategy

Why I looked at this

A category page, a product grid, and a product detail page each make a different kind of promise to a shopper. I wanted to see how Centrepoint's Women's store compares to three very different competitors — Namshi (regional fashion specialist), Myntra (brand-depth marketplace), and Shein (global fast-fashion marketplace) — not to rank them, but to find specific, adoptable fixes Centrepoint could ship.

The finding: filter count doesn't track catalogue size the way you'd expect. Myntra carries ten times Namshi's SKU count but runs the shallowest filter panel of the four — it bets on brand depth (3,000+ brands) to do the narrowing work that attribute filters usually do.

Each retailer leads the product page with a different lever: Centrepoint stacks urgency with loyalty points (Shukrans) and Tabby installments; Namshi runs two countdown timers at once — a delivery window and a flash-sale clock — plus a Premium badge; Myntra goes discount-led, struck-through MRP and a 78% OFF badge with the size chart linked before size selection; Shein leads with social proof, putting star rating and comment count directly under the title, before price.

Recommendations

Things we should adopt

  1. Fix the review-volume gap, not the review feature.

    Centrepoint's sampled PDP had the review function live but zero reviews on it. Recommend a post-purchase nudge through email or app notification, prompting specifically for fit, size, and quality feedback — review volume is the actual gap, not the feature itself.

    Benchmark signal: Shein's visible review / fit-consensus layer

  2. Surface delivery promises earlier.

    Centrepoint already shows estimated delivery ("Get it Tomorrow," or a specific date) on the PDP. Test bringing that same signal onto the PLP as a badge, so delivery speed factors into the product choice before a shopper ever opens the detail page.

    Benchmark signal: Namshi's delivery visibility + Centrepoint's existing PDP capability

  3. Add model details for better fit visualization.

    Surface model height, size worn, and fit notes closer to the size selector, backed by a clear size guide and garment measurements — giving shoppers more confidence in fit before they commit to a size and add to bag.
